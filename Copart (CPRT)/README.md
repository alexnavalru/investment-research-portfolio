# Copart (CPRT) — A High-Quality Vehicle-Auction Franchise at a More Disciplined Entry Price

**Research date:** October 19, 2026  
**Status:** Watchlist / active research; position ownership not assumed  
**Preferred entry price:** **$20/share**  
**Reference valuation price:** **~$27.8/share**, approximating the October 16, 2026 close  
**Case type:** Quality compounder / temporary operational setback / acquisition integration  

## 1. Business overview

Copart operates an online vehicle remarketing platform. Its core business is selling damaged, salvage and total-loss vehicles for insurance companies; complementary sellers include dealers, banks, rental/fleet operators and private owners. It combines online bidding, a global buyer network, physical yards, vehicle pickup, storage, titles and logistics.

**Growth strategy:** Management is focused on three complementary priorities:

1. **International insurance expansion:** grow salvage-auction operations in existing markets and enter new geographies.
2. **U.S. Whole Car expansion:** auction complete, generally usable used vehicles, not necessarily accident-damaged or declared total losses, for dealers and commercial consignors.
3. **Technology and client services:** develop inspection, valuation, logistics and digital tools that improve the experience and outcomes for sellers and buyers.

**How ACV fits:** Copart acquired **ACV Auctions on October 16, 2026**. ACV accelerates the **U.S. Whole Car** strategy through its established digital dealer-auction marketplace, dealer relationships and buyer base. Its inspection and valuation technology also supports the **technology and services** pillar, while Copart can contribute its facilities, transportation capabilities and international buyer network. The combined capabilities may create cross-border opportunities, although international expansion of ACV itself is not an announced base-case assumption.

**Two principal revenue types must not be confused:**

- **Service/agency:** Copart typically does not own the vehicle; it earns auction, processing, transportation, storage and related fees. Sale proceeds remitted to the consignor are **not** Copart revenue. This is the high-quality, asset-supported fee model.
- **Purchased-vehicle/principal:** Copart buys a vehicle and resells it; the gross resale price is recorded as revenue, with a corresponding cost of vehicle sales. Higher gross revenue does not necessarily mean proportionately higher economic profit.

## 2. Investment thesis and potential catalysts

1. **A genuine network advantage:** Copart operates **286 facilities worldwide** (FY2026), serving all 50 U.S. states and buyers in 185+ countries. Its yard network, towing, storage and insurer relationships are expensive to replicate: suitable land requires zoning and permits, while environmental requirements and local opposition can delay expansion.
2. **Long-run total-loss tailwind:** higher repair severity, car complexity, parts/labor costs and rentals support more vehicles being declared economic total losses over time. The Q4 FY2026 call cited 23.3% industry total-loss frequency in calendar Q2 2026 versus 22.4% the year before.
3. **International opportunity:** Q4 FY2026 international unit sales grew 10.0% and insurance units grew 11.2%; management intends to expand existing operations and enter additional European markets. This offsets some, not all, U.S. weakness.
4. **Whole-car optionality via ACV:** Copart paid approximately **$1.9bn in cash** for a scaled digital dealer-to-dealer auction network. Cross-platform buyers, franchise-dealer relationships, inspection technology, logistics and Copart's facilities can improve both networks if integrated successfully.
5. **Substantial balance-sheet resilience:** Copart ended FY2026 with **$4.5bn in liquidity and almost no financial debt**. Following the ACV acquisition, our FY2027 forecast includes **$3.6bn in total liquidity** and **$3.4bn in net cash after financial debt**, equivalent to approximately **12.9% of a ~$26bn reference market capitalization**.
6. **Strong free-cash-flow generation:** Copart generated approximately **$1.3bn in FY2026 FCF (CFO less CAPEX)**, providing capacity for **share repurchases or inorganic growth through M&A**.
7. **Meaningful buyback capacity:** Copart executes share repurchases selectively rather than on a fixed schedule. I project **$450m of buybacks in FY2027**, with scope to buy back more given its strong liquidity.
8. **Return of a long-term CEO:** **Jay Adair returned as CEO on July 31, 2026**, replacing Jeff Liaw after previously leading Copart. During his July 2026 investor conference, Adair described the appointment as long term and said he intends to lead for **another 10-plus years**.
9. **Founder-family economic alignment:** Founder **Willis Johnson (~5.8%)** and his son-in-law, CEO **Jay Adair (~3.2%)**, have substantial beneficial ownership. Some shares are held indirectly. A new **20m-option grant approved for Adair in October 2026**, subject to shareholder approval, comprises four 5m-share tranches with **strikes ranging from $26.27 to $52.54**, vesting after five years and **expiring October 13, 2036**.

## 4. ACV Auctions — acquisition, valuation and strategic fit

**Business and rationale:** Copart mainly handles insurer salvage vehicles, often collecting and storing them at its yards; **ACV Auctions specializes in usable used cars sold by dealerships, often inspected and auctioned digitally while still at the dealer's lot**. Buying ACV accelerates Copart's **U.S. Whole Car** expansion and adds franchise-dealer relationships, vehicle inspection/data tools and technology that can benefit from Copart's yards, logistics and international buyer network.

**Transaction and consolidation:** Announced September 10 and completed **October 16, 2026**, at **$10.50 per ACV share in cash**, or approximately **$1,900m equity value**. It was funded with Copart's existing liquidity, without issuing new Copart shares as purchase consideration. ACV retains its brand and operating team. Copart consolidates approximately **9.5 months in FY2027** and **12 months in FY2028**.

## 9. Valuation — why I prefer a $20 entry

I prefer to wait for a cheaper entry in a strong auction franchise facing U.S. insurer-volume weakness and ACV integration risk. At $20, the earnings and FCF multiples offer a substantially **larger margin of safety** than at the current reference price, provided the underlying business remains healthy.

Copart's **$63.84 all-time closing high (May 16, 2025)** implies a **68.7% decline at $20**. Such a drawdown would approach its **72.5% fall from late 2001 to March 2003**, during the post-dot-com bear market, when company revenue growth slowed and profits stagnated.

## 10. Risks and what would disprove the thesis

1. **Sector weakness:** declining U.S. collision-claim frequency, weaker insurer salvage volumes, lower used-car prices or reduced export demand could weigh on units, proceeds and margins even if total-loss rates rise.
2. **Further insurer contract losses:** losing additional large carriers to IAA or accepting worse commercial terms could indicate a structural weakening of Copart's U.S. network advantage.
3. **ACV integration and disappointing synergies:** Copart paid around **$1.9bn** for a lower-margin, GAAP-loss-making business. Weak dealer adoption, integration difficulties or synergies below expectations could depress returns and cash generation.
4. **Poor capital allocation:** Copart carried out a major **$1.6bn share buyback in FY2026 at an average $37.6/share**, a demanding valuation **while U.S. insurance volumes and operating costs were already deteriorating**. Further expensive buybacks or acquisitions could waste otherwise substantial cash resources.
5. **Valuation compression:** a premium franchise can still suffer a prolonged derating if organic growth stalls or margins deteriorate, regardless of ACV-driven reported revenue growth.
6. **Governance:** family influence and the incentive structure of management require monitoring, especially following the CEO transition.
