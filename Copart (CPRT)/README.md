# Copart (CPRT) — A High-Quality Vehicle-Auction Franchise at a More Disciplined Entry Price

**Research date:** October 19, 2026  
**Status:** Watchlist / active research; position ownership not assumed  
**Preferred entry price:** **$20.00/share** (not an automatic buy order)  
**Reference share price:** **$27.87**, October 16, 2026 close (TIKR reference)  
**Valuation horizon:** FY2028 (fiscal year ending July 31, 2028)  
**Case type:** Quality compounder / temporary operational setback / acquisition integration  
**Acquisition status:** ACV Auctions acquired on October 16, 2026  

> **Core view:** Copart operates an attractive, difficult-to-replicate salvage-vehicle auction network with significant cash generation, international buyer liquidity, land/logistics capacity and founder-family ownership. However, FY2026 exposed a real U.S. insurance-volume and cost-per-unit problem, while the $1.9bn ACV purchase brings growth but weaker near-term profit conversion. I want **a $20 entry price**, providing a more attractive margin of safety than the approximately $28 market reference. A low price by itself does **not** override deterioration in the core business.

**Supporting model:** [CPRT — Financial Model & Valuation Snapshot](./MODEL_SNAPSHOT.md).

## 1. Business overview

Copart operates an online vehicle remarketing platform. Its core business is selling damaged, salvage and total-loss vehicles for insurance companies; complementary sellers include dealers, banks, rental/fleet operators and private owners. It combines online bidding, a global buyer network, physical yards, vehicle pickup, storage, titles and logistics.

**Two principal revenue types must not be confused:**

- **Service/agency:** Copart typically does not own the vehicle; it earns auction, processing, transportation, storage and related fees. Sale proceeds remitted to the consignor are **not** Copart revenue. This is the high-quality, asset-supported fee model.
- **Purchased-vehicle/principal:** Copart buys a vehicle and resells it; the gross resale price is recorded as revenue, with a corresponding cost of vehicle sales. Higher gross revenue does not necessarily mean proportionately higher economic profit.

FY2026 **service revenues were $3,969.5m (85.1% of total)** and **vehicle-sale revenues $696.7m (14.9%)**. Pricing/mix and units must therefore be analyzed separately. A higher average selling price can improve auction economics, but gross auction value is not equivalent to reported revenue.

**Why the franchise is hard to replicate:** network effects (more buyers potentially increase seller proceeds; more vehicles attract buyers), 275+ facilities, zoning/permitted land, towing and storage infrastructure, data and bid liquidity, insurer relationships and buyers from over 185 countries. In FY2026, buyers outside the United States bought **38.2% of vehicles sold in U.S. auctions by units**, accounting for **45.7% of the dollars spent**. Both the land network and buyer liquidity are important; describing the company simply as a software marketplace misses a large part of its economics.

**Competitors:** RB Global's IAA is the clearest salvage-auction comparison. ACV, Manheim, OPENLANE and ADESA/Carvana are relevant to *whole-car* wholesale auctions but have materially different operating mixes. Do not compare margins or EV/revenue without adjusting for purchased-vehicle accounting, land intensity and financing services.

## 2. Investment thesis and potential catalysts

1. **A genuine network advantage:** large insurer supply, local yards and international buyer demand can deliver compelling net proceeds for sellers. These assets are difficult to rebuild quickly and explain Copart's historically high margins.
2. **Long-run total-loss tailwind:** higher repair severity, car complexity, parts/labor costs and rentals support more vehicles being declared economic total losses over time. The Q4 FY2026 call cited 23.3% industry total-loss frequency in calendar Q2 2026 versus 22.4% the year before. This tailwind is **not the same as near-term claims-volume growth**.
3. **International opportunity:** Q4 FY2026 international unit sales grew 10% and insurance units grew 11.2%; management intends to expand existing operations and enter additional European markets. This offsets some, not all, U.S. weakness.
4. **Whole-car optionality via ACV:** Copart paid approximately **$1.9bn in cash** for a scaled digital dealer-to-dealer auction network. Cross-platform buyers, franchise-dealer relationships, inspection technology, logistics and Copart's facilities can improve both networks if integrated successfully.
5. **Substantial balance-sheet resilience:** at July 31, 2026 Copart held approximately **$4.49bn cash and held-to-maturity securities**, with no material drawn financial debt. This is the balance **before ACV** and should not be used unadjusted after closing.
6. **Meaningful buyback capacity:** Copart repurchased **43.43m shares for $1.63bn** in FY2026 at $37.63 on average. Future buybacks near $20 would potentially create more per-share value than repurchases at far higher prices—but are discretionary, not guaranteed.
7. **Founder-family economic alignment:** founder Willis Johnson and returning CEO Jay Adair have large beneficial holdings, although some interests are held in trusts, partnerships and foundations and not all represent freely disposable personal wealth.
8. **Operational recovery potential:** management publicly acknowledged elevated U.S. costs per vehicle and says it intends to reduce them. Stabilizing insurance volumes and unit economics, rather than merely reporting ACV-driven sales growth, would be a more convincing catalyst.

**Variant perception:** I am interested in a strong underlying franchise during a difficult operating year, but I do **not** presume the U.S. volume decline is temporary. The thesis requires measurable evidence that customer concentration, tender losses and competitive pricing are not permanently eroding its insurer franchise.

## 3. FY2026 operating problems — why the stock is under pressure

FY2026 consolidated sales grew just **0.4%**, operating income fell **2.6%**, and net income attributable to Copart fell **4.4%**. U.S. revenue declined while international operations grew.

During **Q4 FY2026**, as reported in the September 10 earnings call:

| Operating indicator | Q4 FY2026 year-on-year |
|---|---:|
| Global vehicles sold | **-2.9%** |
| U.S. vehicles sold | **-5.7%** |
| U.S. insurance units | **-7.5%** |
| International vehicles sold | **+10.0%** |
| International insurance units | **+11.2%** |
| Global insurance average selling prices | **+3.1%** |
| Global Q4 gross margin | **41.8%** |
| U.S. facility-related cost **per unit** | **+14.2%** |

For **FY2026 overall**, U.S. insurance unit volumes fell approximately **8%** and total U.S. units sold approximately **6.9%**. Adair disclosed that **excluding one lost insurer customer, U.S. insurance assignments would have been +2.3% in Q4**. This is management's counterfactual and uses *assignments*, not the same metric as sold units; it does not erase the actual loss or quantify permanent share loss.

Management cited declining collision-claim frequency (approximately **-3.4%**), greater total-loss severity and investment in new services/physical capacity. CFO Leah Stearns indicated the majority of the recent facility-cost increase came from services such as long-haul delivery and added capacity; about **$17m** of the YoY facility-cost increase related to long-haul delivery. These products can ultimately generate margin, but the company must demonstrate that revenue and throughput justify the cost base.

**Bearish interpretation:** U.S. insurers are becoming more price-sensitive; IAA may be winning accounts; the apparently stable historical moat is weakening. **More constructive interpretation:** one large contract loss, falling collision claims and a temporary investment phase explain much of the pressure, while higher average proceeds and international buyers preserve the competitive advantage. I will test both against coming quarterly assignments, seller wins/losses and margin trends.

## 5. ACV Auctions — acquisition, valuation and strategic fit

**Announced:** September 10, 2026. **Completed:** October 16, 2026. **Price:** $10.50 per ACVA share in cash, approximately **$1.9bn equity value**, a 45% premium to ACV's unaffected August 10 share price. Copart funded the offer with existing cash. ACV retains its brand and management; the group can integrate buyers, transportation and yard use without merging both customer-facing platforms.

ACV inspects and auctions used, generally serviceable vehicles for franchised and independent dealers. Core offerings include digital dealer auctions, vehicle-inspection/reporting data, ACV Transportation, financing (ACV Capital), ACV MAX and VIPER. ACV sold over 800,000 vehicles in 2025 with approximately $10bn merchandise value, **not $10bn of revenue**.

**Price versus profitability (indicative):**

- ACV FY2025 revenue: approximately **$760m**; FY2026 standalone revenue guidance: **$845m–$855m**, midpoint $850m.
- FY2026 standalone guidance: **$73m–$77m adjusted EBITDA** and **$44m–$49m GAAP net loss**.
- Using approximately **$242m cash and $205m financial debt** at June 2026, illustrative enterprise value is **$1.86bn** ($1.90bn + $205m - $242m), excluding closing adjustments and other equity-award consideration.
- This implies about **2.2x FY2026E revenue** and **24.8x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA** at the midpoint; the **FY2025 FCF yield on the equity purchase price was low**. These are not bargain multiples on current profits.
- ACV's H1 2026 revenue was **$418.1m**; GAAP operating loss **$15.3m**, CFO **$41.0m**, capex **$4.9m physical + $18.5m capitalized software**, resulting in **$17.6m CFO-less-total-capex FCF**. Ignore capitalized software and the FCF is overstated.

**ACV's different projection sets:** its **August 2026 public guidance** projected **$850m FY2026 revenue and $75m adjusted EBITDA** (midpoints). The **September 2026 Schedule 14D-9**, prepared for the sale process on a standalone basis, included a *different* management scenario:

| ACV standalone, December year-end | CY2026E | CY2027E | CY2028E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue ($m) | **857** | **964** | **1,116** |
| Adjusted EBITDA excluding SBC ($m) | **78** | **123** | **187** |
| Adjusted EBIT **after** SBC and D&A ($m) | **-15** | **20** | **73** |
| Unlevered FCF ($m) | n/a | **-97** | **-69** |

These transaction projections were **not guidance** and assume ACV remains standalone. Particularly important: ACV's non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA excludes **stock-based compensation (SBC)**, which is a real economic cost to shareholders. FY2026 standalone guidance anticipated approximately **$63m of SBC**. The negative projected *unlevered* FCF underscores working-capital/credit-growth demands and must not be casually reconciled to positive CFO-minus-capex.

**Potential synergies:** buyer liquidity, direct access to franchise dealers, better net proceeds, cross-border demand, yard staging, title/logistics capabilities and inspection technology. Copart's call discussed training, ACV-specific facility areas and continued technology investment. **Management has not quantified a reliable net synergy target**; the amounts in my model are illustrative.

**EPS guidance nuance:** the September announcement described the purchase as EPS-accretive in **Copart FY2028 and beyond**. In Q4 Q&A, the CFO explained that FY2028 was the first full fiscal year expected after an uncertain close date, and used break-even/initial accretion language. The release did **not** quantify EPS, amortization, integration costs or give a detailed GAAP/non-GAAP reconciliation. As of October 16 the acquisition has actually closed: approximately **9.5 months of ACV fall in Copart FY2027**, and the full 12 months in FY2028.

**Confidence levels:** relatively high in FY2026 audited results, acquisition timing and transaction price; medium in near-term ACV revenues; **low in synergy EBIT, purchase-price amortization, working capital, goodwill and 2028 profitability**. In particular, the pro forma CFO scenarios have not been reconciled to ACV's standalone projected negative unlevered FCF; this is a key downside sensitivity.

## 8. Valuation — why I prefer a $20 entry

The **$20 entry** is my intended valuation discipline, **not** an assertion that the current market must trade there. Use 926.33m actual outstanding shares for spot-equity capitalization and approximately 915m prospective diluted shares for the FY2028 valuation; never use FY2026 weighted-average shares for the current market cap.

**At $20/share** (spot share-count reference):
- Equity capitalization = **$18.53bn** (926.33m × $20).
- Cash immediately after acquisition, **simplified June/July balance-sheet proxy**: $4.49bn pre-deal liquidity - $1.90bn cash purchase + ~$0.24bn ACV cash = **$2.83bn**; less ~$0.21bn acquired financial debt implies **~$2.63bn net cash**. The actual October 16 cash/debt bridge and closing consideration can differ.
- Illustrative **pro forma EV ~$15.90bn** (market cap less ~$2.63bn net cash), equivalent to **~7.0x modeled FY2028 EBITDA**.
- **FY2028 P/E ~11.3x** ($20 / $1.77), **P/FCF ~13.8x** ($20 / $1.45), FCF equity yield **~7.3%**. This FCF yield is *before* additional lending-book investment.

**At the $27.87 October 16 reference:** the corresponding FY2028 P/E is **~15.7x**, P/FCF **~19.2x**, and pro forma EV/FY2028 EBITDA **~10.3x**, on unchanged acquisition-close balance assumptions. Both price comparisons use the same model—not synchronized future market quotes.

### FY2028 enterprise-value sensitivity

A simplified equity bridge: **price = (FY2028 EBITDA × EV/EBITDA + FY2028 net cash) / average diluted shares**. Scenarios deliberately change multiple, operating performance, net cash and share count; none is a management target. FY2028 year-end net cash is applied to FY2028 earnings as an illustrative normalized valuation, not as a precise present-value DCF.

| Scenario | FY2028 EBITDA | EV/EBITDA | Net cash | Diluted shares | Implied equity/share | vs. $20 entry |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Bear**: prolonged U.S. volume losses; little ACV leverage | $1,900m | 7.0x | $3,200m | 925m | **$17.84** | **-10.8%** |
| **Base**: modest salvage recovery + some ACV synergy | $2,256m | 9.0x | $4,125m | 915m | **$26.70** | **+33.5%** |
| **Bull**: unit recovery, strong ACV execution | $2,450m | 11.0x | $4,300m | 910m | **$34.34** | **+71.7%** |

The bear is **not a hard downside floor**: a deeper loss of insurer share, weaker FCF, expensive buybacks, acquisition failure or multiple compression could justify prices well below $17–18. The bull requires execution not yet proved.

The base-case upside from **$27.87** is approximately **-4.2%** over the indicated valuation reference, before the time value of waiting. That is why I would prefer to **wait for $20 rather than chase**. At $20 the model offers a more comfortable potential reward versus a conventional operating downside case, provided the fundamentals remain intact. No dividend or buyback execution is guaranteed.

**Cross-check:** at $20 the **FY2028 modeled FCF of $1.33bn / spot $18.53bn equity** implies approximately **7.2% FCF yield**; using **$1.45 FCF per modeled diluted share** produces around **7.3%**, a harmless discrepancy due to different share-count bases. Do not claim that the capex-adjusted FCF equals cash distributable to common shareholders if ACV's finance receivables require additional funding.

## 9. Risks and what would disprove the thesis

1. **Permanent U.S. insurance-share losses:** more named insurer contract losses, persistent negative domestic assignment volumes relative to peers, or price concessions required to retain insurers would undermine the moat case.
2. **Higher cost per unit / margin damage:** new services and capacity may not earn sufficient incremental returns. Watch facility cost per vehicle, revenue per unit and U.S. EBIT margin, **not only** ASP.
3. **ACV integration and overpayment:** $1.9bn for a GAAP-loss-making, adjusted-metric-heavy business. Weak platform adoption, inadequate synergies, SBC, additional amortization or credit losses could make ROIC disappointing.
4. **ACV Capital credit/working-capital risk:** floorplan/wholesale financing draws liquidity; operating cash flow and unlevered FCF are not identical to CFO-minus-capex.
5. **Capital allocation:** Copart's large buyback at ~$37.63 versus my $20 desired entry illustrates the risk that an otherwise great business can destroy value by deploying cash at the wrong price.
6. **Economic sensitivity:** collision frequency can decline even when total-loss frequency rises. Lower used-car values, export restrictions, insurance cost pressures or recession can change volumes, ASPs and buyers.
7. **Insider/governance issues:** family influence, permitted share pledging and modification of departing executive awards.
8. **Valuation compression:** even a high-ROIC network can trade at a lower multiple for years; my base valuation is *not* a near-term guaranteed target.
9. **Accounting risk:** newly allocated ACV intangibles, goodwill impairment, lease liabilities and contingent equity awards can change reported EBIT, D&A and share count versus this preliminary model.

### Specific quarterly checks

- **Core insurance:** U.S. insurer assignments/sold units, claims frequency, new customer wins/losses, concentration, IAA share dynamics.
- **Per-unit economics:** service revenue per unit, global and U.S. ASP, costs per unit, gross margin and EBITDA margin.
- **ACV separately:** organic dealer units, GMV, revenue per unit, SBC, EBIT after SBC, cash capex and capitalized software, loan growth and realized synergies.
- **Balance/returns:** actual post-close net cash, PPA goodwill and intangible amortization, net investment in finance receivables, FCF and acquisition ROIC.
- **Shareholders:** repurchase price/quantity, shares **outstanding** versus diluted **weighted average**, option exercises and converted ACV awards.
- **Management:** updated FY2027 compensation and insider beneficial ownership when the new proxy is available.

**I would reconsider the $20 entry thesis** if U.S. customer losses continue, group EBIT remains structurally below the FY2026 run rate, ACV's adjusted EBITDA never converts into economic cash returns, or the balance-sheet cash is spent on low-return M&A. A falling stock price alone is not a catalyst.
