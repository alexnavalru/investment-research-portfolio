# Copart (CPRT) — A High-Quality Vehicle-Auction Franchise at a More Disciplined Entry Price

**Research date:** October 19, 2026  
**Status:** Watchlist / active research; position ownership not assumed  
**Preferred entry price:** **$20/share**  
**Reference share price:** **$27.9**, October 16, 2026 close  
**Case type:** Quality compounder / temporary operational setback / acquisition integration  

## 1. Business overview

Copart operates an online vehicle remarketing platform. Its core business is selling damaged, salvage and total-loss vehicles for insurance companies; complementary sellers include dealers, banks, rental/fleet operators and private owners. It combines online bidding, a global buyer network, physical yards, vehicle pickup, storage, titles and logistics.

**Growth strategy:** Management is focused on three complementary priorities:

1. **International insurance expansion:** grow salvage-auction operations in existing markets and enter new geographies.
2. **U.S. Whole Car expansion:** auction complete, generally usable used vehicles, not necessarily accident-damaged or declared total losses, for dealers and commercial consignors.
3. **Technology and client services:** develop inspection, valuation, logistics and digital tools that improve the experience and outcomes for sellers and buyers.

**How ACV fits:** Copart acquired **ACV Auctions on October 16, 2026**. ACV accelerates the **U.S. Whole Car** strategy through its established digital dealer-auction marketplace, dealer relationships and buyer base. Its inspection and valuation technology also supports the **technology and services** pillar, while Copart can contribute its facilities, transportation capabilities and international buyer network. The combined capabilities may create cross-border opportunities, although international expansion of ACV itself is not an announced base-case assumption.

**Two principal revenue types must not be confused:**

- **Service/agency:** Copart typically does not own the vehicle; it earns auction, processing, transportation, storage and related fees. Sale proceeds remitted to the consignor are **not** Copart revenue. This is the high-quality, asset-supported fee model.
- **Purchased-vehicle/principal:** Copart buys a vehicle and resells it; the gross resale price is recorded as revenue, with a corresponding cost of vehicle sales. Higher gross revenue does not necessarily mean proportionately higher economic profit.

## 2. Investment thesis and potential catalysts

1. **A genuine network advantage:** Copart operates **286 facilities worldwide** (FY2026), serving all 50 U.S. states and buyers in 185+ countries. Its yard network, towing, storage and insurer relationships are expensive to replicate: suitable land requires zoning and permits, while environmental requirements and local opposition can delay expansion. International buyers acquired **38.2% of U.S. auction units**, representing **45.7% of their purchase value** in FY2026. This combination of physical scale and buyer liquidity is a major competitive barrier, although IAA also operates a large network.
2. **Long-run total-loss tailwind:** higher repair severity, car complexity, parts/labor costs and rentals support more vehicles being declared economic total losses over time. The Q4 FY2026 call cited 23.3% industry total-loss frequency in calendar Q2 2026 versus 22.4% the year before. This tailwind is **not the same as near-term claims-volume growth**.
3. **International opportunity:** Q4 FY2026 international unit sales grew 10.0% and insurance units grew 11.2%; management intends to expand existing operations and enter additional European markets. This offsets some, not all, U.S. weakness.
4. **Whole-car optionality via ACV:** Copart paid approximately **$1.9bn in cash** for a scaled digital dealer-to-dealer auction network. Cross-platform buyers, franchise-dealer relationships, inspection technology, logistics and Copart's facilities can improve both networks if integrated successfully.
5. **Substantial balance-sheet resilience:** Copart ended FY2026 with **$4.5bn in liquidity and almost no financial debt**. After the ACV purchase and our modeled cash flow and buybacks, I project **$3.4bn FY2027 / $4.1bn FY2028 net cash**, equivalent to roughly **13.0% / 16.0%** of a ~$26bn reference market capitalization.
6. **Strong free-cash-flow generation:** Despite declining U.S. insurance volumes, Copart generated **$1.3bn in FY2026 FCF (CFO less capex)**. Our model estimates roughly **$1.3bn in FY2027 and $1.3bn in FY2028**. Continued FCF can rebuild cash after ACV, fund investments and support share repurchases, potentially increasing per-share value. The estimates remain sensitive to normalized capex, working capital and ACV Capital's financing needs.
7. **Meaningful buyback capacity:** Copart bought back **43.4m shares for $1.6bn in FY2026** at an average **$37.6/share**. My model assumes **$450m annually at $30/share** in FY2027–FY2028, supporting per-share value; the company could repurchase more but has historically preferred financial flexibility. This is an analyst assumption, not guidance.
8. **Return of a long-term CEO:** **Jay Adair returned as CEO on July 31, 2026**, replacing Jeff Liaw after previously leading Copart. During his July 2026 investor conference, Adair described the appointment as long term and said he intends to lead for **another 10-plus years**. His renewed focus on international insurance, U.S. Whole Car and technology may support execution, though the planned tenure is an intention rather than a guarantee.
9. **Founder-family economic alignment:** Founder **Willis Johnson (~5.8%)** and his son-in-law, CEO **Jay Adair (~3.2%)**, have substantial beneficial ownership (FY2025 proxy). Some shares are held indirectly. A new **20m-option grant for Adair approved in October 2026**, subject to shareholder approval, could increase incentive alignment but also dilution.

CFO Leah Stearns said much of the additional facility expense was associated with offerings such as long-haul delivery; approximately **$17m** of the Q4 year-on-year cost increase was related to that service. These initiatives may contribute revenue and profits, but management must show the returns justify the associated unit costs.

## 4. ACV Auctions — acquisition, valuation and strategic fit

**Business and rationale:** Copart mainly handles insurer salvage vehicles, often collecting and storing them at its yards; **ACV Auctions specializes in usable used cars sold by dealerships, often inspected and auctioned digitally while still at the dealer's lot**. Buying ACV accelerates Copart's **U.S. Whole Car** expansion and adds franchise-dealer relationships, vehicle inspection/data tools and technology that can benefit from Copart's yards, logistics and international buyer network.

**Transaction and consolidation:** Announced September 10 and completed **October 16, 2026**, at **$10.50 per ACV share in cash**, or approximately **$1,900m equity value**. It was funded with Copart's existing liquidity, without issuing new Copart shares as purchase consideration. ACV retains its brand and operating team. Copart consolidates approximately **9.5 months in FY2027** and **12 months in FY2028**.

## 9. Valuation — why I prefer a $20 entry

**Interest-income normalization matters.** FY2026's $182m net interest income was supported by a large securities/cash portfolio. The approximately $1.9bn cash purchase of ACV sacrifices interest income; ACV also has financing activities and debt. This reduces near-term EPS even if the auction business holds up. Interest belongs **below EBIT**.

The **$20 entry** is my intended valuation discipline, **not** an assertion that the current market must trade there. Use 926.3m actual outstanding shares for spot-equity capitalization and approximately 915m prospective diluted shares for the FY2028 valuation; never use FY2026 weighted-average shares for the current market cap.

**At $20/share** (spot share-count reference):
- Equity capitalization = **$18.5bn** (926.3m × $20).
- Cash immediately after acquisition, **simplified June/July balance-sheet proxy**: ~$4.5bn pre-deal liquidity - ~$1.9bn cash purchase + ~$0.2bn ACV cash = **~$2.8bn**; less ~$0.2bn acquired financial debt implies **~$2.6bn net cash**. The actual October 16 cash/debt bridge and closing consideration can differ.
- Illustrative **pro forma EV ~$15.9bn** (market cap less ~$2.6bn net cash), equivalent to **~7.0x modeled FY2028 EBITDA**.
- **FY2028 P/E ~11.3x** ($20 / $1.77), **P/FCF ~13.8x** ($20 / $1.45), FCF equity yield **~7.3%**. This FCF yield is *before* additional lending-book investment.

**At the $27.9 October 16 reference:** the corresponding FY2028 P/E is **~15.7x**, P/FCF **~19.2x**, and pro forma EV/FY2028 EBITDA **~10.3x**, on unchanged acquisition-close balance assumptions. Both price comparisons use the same model—not synchronized future market quotes.

### FY2028 enterprise-value sensitivity

A simplified equity bridge: **price = (FY2028 EBITDA × EV/EBITDA + FY2028 net cash) / average diluted shares**. Scenarios deliberately change multiple, operating performance, net cash and share count; none is a management target. FY2028 year-end net cash is applied to FY2028 earnings as an illustrative normalized valuation, not as a precise present-value DCF.

| Scenario | FY2028 EBITDA | EV/EBITDA | Net cash | Diluted shares | Implied equity/share | vs. $20 entry |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Bear**: prolonged U.S. volume losses; little ACV leverage | $1,900m | 7.0x | $3,200m | 925m | **$17.8** | **-10.8%** |
| **Base**: modest salvage recovery + some ACV synergy | $2,256m | 9.0x | $4,125m | 915m | **$26.7** | **+33.5%** |
| **Bull**: unit recovery, strong ACV execution | $2,450m | 11.0x | $4,300m | 910m | **$34.3** | **+71.7%** |

The bear is **not a hard downside floor**: a deeper loss of insurer share, weaker FCF, expensive buybacks, acquisition failure or multiple compression could justify prices well below $17–18. The bull requires execution not yet proved.

The base-case upside from **$27.9** is approximately **-4.3%** over the indicated valuation reference, before the time value of waiting. That is why I would prefer to **wait for $20 rather than chase**. At $20 the model offers a more comfortable potential reward versus a conventional operating downside case, provided the fundamentals remain intact. No dividend or buyback execution is guaranteed.

**Cross-check:** at $20 the **FY2028 modeled FCF of ~$1.3bn / spot ~$18.5bn equity** implies approximately **7.2% FCF yield**; using **$1.45 FCF per modeled diluted share** produces around **7.3%**, a harmless discrepancy due to different share-count bases. Do not claim that the capex-adjusted FCF equals cash distributable to common shareholders if ACV's finance receivables require additional funding.

## 10. Risks and what would disprove the thesis

1. **Sector weakness:** declining U.S. collision-claim frequency, weaker insurer salvage volumes, lower used-car prices or reduced export demand could weigh on units, proceeds and margins even if total-loss rates rise.
2. **Further insurer contract losses:** losing additional large carriers to IAA or accepting worse commercial terms could indicate a structural weakening of Copart's U.S. network advantage.
3. **ACV integration and disappointing synergies:** Copart paid around **$1.9bn** for a lower-margin, GAAP-loss-making business. Weak dealer adoption, integration difficulties or synergies below expectations could depress returns and cash generation.
4. **Poor capital allocation:** Copart carried out a major **$1.6bn share buyback in FY2026 at an average $37.6/share**, a demanding valuation **while U.S. insurance volumes and operating costs were already deteriorating**. Further expensive buybacks or acquisitions could waste otherwise substantial cash resources.
5. **Valuation compression:** a premium franchise can still suffer a prolonged derating if organic growth stalls or margins deteriorate, regardless of ACV-driven reported revenue growth.
