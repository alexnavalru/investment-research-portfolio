## 2. Distributor working capital
Definitions: **DSO** receivables days (receivables/sales×365); **DIO** inventory days (inventory/COGS×365); **DPO** payable days (payables/COGS×365); **CCC = DSO+DIO−DPO**. Trade **WC = receivables + inventory − trade payables**, not all current assets less current liabilities.

| FY | DSO | DIO | DPO | CCC | Trade WC ($m) | WC/revenues |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2017 | 35 | 133 | 44 | 124 | 2619 | 26.9% |
| 2018 | 34 | 130 | 43 | 121 | 3048 | 25.7% |
| 2019 | 33 | 134 | 45 | 122 | 2961 | 23.7% |
| 2020 | 35 | 135 | 49 | 121 | 2556 | 22.0% |
| 2021 | 30 | 118 | 50 | 98 | 2508 | 19.2% |
| 2022 | 30 | 129 | 61 | 98 | 2411 | 18.8% |
| 2023 | 30 | 135 | 69 | 96 | 2638 | **19.0%** |
| 2024 | 30 | 136 | 75 | 91 | 2499 | 18.1% |
| 2025 | 31 | 144 | 85 | 90 | 2522 | 18.5% |

**Takeaway:** DPO grew **44→85 days** whereas DIO got worse (**133→144 days**), so CCC improved **124→90 days** mainly through *supplier payment terms*. Working capital/revenue improved **26.9%→18.5%**, but this is vulnerable if suppliers tighten terms, while inventory continues growing. 2025 workbook shows **~$481m** supplier-finance balances, worth separately verifying. **Changes in WC are already included in CFO**; do not subtract the whole $2.52bn WC balance again when computing FCF.

Q2 2026 **gross debt $3,961m – cash $301m = net debt $3,660m**. Apply $350m FY26 debt repayment to get $3,310m, assuming cash balances unchanged. Approx closing leverage FY26 **2.35x** and FY27 **2.09x** on modeled EBITDA. **Timing reconciliation (do not double-count H1):** FY2026 modeled FCF **$660m − $310m annual dividends = $350m** before other capital uses; guidance minimum **$625m − $310m = $315m**. But at the **Q2 starting debt balance**, official **H1 2026 FCF was −$36m**, with **$154m dividends and $53m repurchases already incurred**. Modeled H2 FCF is thus **$660m − (−$36m) = $696m**, less about **$156m remaining dividends = $540m H2** available before other uses. Choosing only **$350m net debt decline from Q2** in the SOTP leaves **~$190m for future buybacks/other capital spending, FX or cash/financing movements**; these uses are not forecast individually and the bridge is **not fully reconciled**. Do not assert zero share buybacks during FY2026. Disposal proceeds are excluded.

**Entry / interpretation:** On rounded inputs these are around **8–8.5x equity P/FCF** and **14–14.3x EV/FCF including net debt**. Because CFO/FCF already includes net interest paid, EV/FCF is a **mixed enterprise/levered-cash-flow multiple**, best used only as a supplementary leverage-aware reference. It does not mean the company is especially cheap. My preferred entry is **~$20**, with **no formal target price**; the SOTP range merely tests whether capital actions and restructuring could unlock value. The share price has experienced a ~**58.8%** historical drawdown on a total-return series, comparable in scale with 2008 (~62.8%) and 2020 (~68.0%): PortfoliosLab.

**Near-term Q3 risk:** LKQ reports on 29 Oct 2026. German ERP disruption and weak U.S. repair demand could cause another guidance reduction. U.S. policy rates rose 25bp to 3.75–4.0% in September 2026. Waiting may reveal a better entry; risks may also prove already priced in. A possible dividend cut is not in the base model: despite near-term share-price risk, reallocating part of **$310m dividends** to debt could accelerate deleveraging if sustainable FCF remains.
