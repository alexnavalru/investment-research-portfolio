# Rackspace Technology (RXT) — Leveraged Enterprise AI Infrastructure Turnaround

**Case type:** Special situation / leveraged turnaround / enterprise AI infrastructure  
**Status:** Speculative watchlist / active research (no position disclosed)  
**Reference price:** **$3.15**, October 14, 2026 closing price  
**Valuation horizon:** 2028–2029 normalized/run-rate EBITDA  
**Last updated:** October 15, 2026

## 1. Business overview

Rackspace Technology (NASDAQ: RXT) provides managed cloud infrastructure, hosting, security and technology services for enterprise customers. Its two main segments are **Private Cloud**, where it operates dedicated or hosted infrastructure and mission-critical environments, and **Public Cloud**, where it manages workloads and cloud services across third-party platforms such as AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud.

The company earns revenue by hosting, managing, securing and modernizing customers' technology environments. Its footprint includes data-center capacity, power, cooling, infrastructure specialists and longstanding relationships with regulated organizations in sectors such as healthcare, financial services and government.

Historically, low-margin cloud infrastructure resale, competition from hyperscalers, high fixed costs and substantial debt have weighed on performance. Management is repositioning Rackspace around **Enterprise AI, private and sovereign cloud, and managed inference**, aiming to provide not just infrastructure but the integration and ongoing operation of AI workloads.

## 2. Investment thesis and potential catalysts

1. **Existing data centers and available power.** Rackspace intends to repurpose capacity already in its infrastructure footprint for higher-value AI workloads. Greater utilization could spread fixed costs over more revenue and improve returns without building all its facilities from scratch.
2. **AMD's planned 30 MW AI deployment.** The roadmap calls for ~2 MW by the end of 2026, 15 MW cumulative in 2027 and 30 MW cumulative in 2028. Management estimates **$450–600m of annual revenue** and **$225–300m+ of adjusted EBITDA** at full deployment and the targeted economics. Executing and contracting those MW is the principal measurable growth catalyst.
3. **Palantir and higher-value enterprise AI services.** Rackspace can combine Palantir software, AMD/NVIDIA compute and its own infrastructure-management capabilities to move from low-margin resale toward secure, regulated, end-to-end AI deployments and inference.
4. **Cost savings and exiting unattractive revenue.** Management plans **$75–85m in annualized workforce-related savings**, although a significant part will be reinvested. Leaving low-margin hosting/colocation and Public Cloud infrastructure resale could improve the revenue mix over time.
5. **Improving commercial visibility.** Rising RPO and expanding relationships with regulated clients suggest demand for managed, long-term infrastructure services. The next test is whether AI-specific contracted capacity becomes profitable revenue and cash flow.
6. **Operating leverage and EBITDA recovery.** The company guides to ~$290m adjusted EBITDA in 2026, while the planned AI buildout creates the possibility of a much higher future run rate. A sustained recovery could materially lift equity value because debt accounts for a large share of enterprise value.
7. **High financial leverage and asymmetric equity returns.** Rackspace's substantial debt can magnify gains for shareholders if EBITDA rises and the business is refinanced successfully, but it also amplifies downside risk if the turnaround disappoints. Debt financing for GPUs, dilution and the 2028 maturities remain critical risks.
8. **Potential sale of Public Cloud.** A hypothetical divestiture of the Public Cloud segment could allow Rackspace to concentrate resources on higher-value Private Cloud and Enterprise AI while using sale proceeds to reduce debt. This is an **unconfirmed strategic possibility, not an announced transaction**; the proceeds, lost earnings and transaction terms would determine whether it creates value.
9. **Apollo's ownership and potential incentives.** Apollo remains a controlling shareholder, with a reported historical equity entry cost above the current share price. Recovering its equity investment could incentivize Apollo to support a higher long-term valuation. **This alignment is not guaranteed:** Apollo's interests in the broader capital structure, including debt, may differ from those of minority shareholders in a restructuring.

## 3. Q1 and Q2 2026 results — brief review

**Q1:** Revenue reached **$678m** (+~2% YoY), adjusted EBITDA was **~$71m** and reported FCF was **-$9m**. Public Cloud grew while Private Cloud declined. Cost control improved, but the underlying private-infrastructure business had not yet stabilized.

**Q2:** Revenue was **$670m** (+~1% YoY), adjusted EBITDA **~$66m** and reported FCF **-$48m**. Private Cloud revenue rose to **$263m**, though a meaningful part of its apparent outperformance came from early recognition of a contract's embedded lease rather than repeatable organic growth. Public Cloud revenue fell to **$407m**.

**2026 guidance:** Management reduced revenue expectations from **$2.60–2.70bn** to **$2.45–2.55bn** and adjusted EBITDA from **$305–315m** to **$285–295m**, partly because of deliberate exits from low-margin activities. It maintained **$50–70m of positive FY2026 FCF**, making the second half especially important.

## 4. AMD: the most quantifiable growth opportunity

The definitive AMD agreement provides a framework for a staged enterprise AI compute deployment, focused particularly on inference, regulated enterprises and sovereign workloads.

| Milestone / metric | Management's plan or estimate |
|---|---:|
| Initial deployment by end-2026 | ~2 MW |
| Initial deployment capex | ~$75m |
| Capacity by end-2027 | 15 MW cumulative |
| Capacity by end-2028 | 30 MW cumulative |
| Annual revenue per deployed MW | $15–20m (illustrative) |
| Annual revenue at 30 MW | $450–600m (illustrative) |
| Adjusted EBITDA margin on AI deployments | 50%+ (target) |
| Implied annual adjusted EBITDA at full capacity | ~$225–300m+ |

**Potential benefit:** Existing sites, electricity access, cooling and operating relationships could let Rackspace commercialize compute capacity faster than an entirely greenfield build. Selling higher-value, governed services on top of compute would be preferable to remaining a low-value bare-metal supplier.

**What is not yet proven:** The 30 MW is a **target, not 30 MW already built or fully contracted**. Deployment approvals, customer commitments, AMD-related offtake conditions, hardware deliveries and funding remain important dependencies. AMD's participation does not amount to an unconditional commitment to buy every planned MW. Rackspace has identified minimum revenue arrangements for the first approved deployment, but this should not be extrapolated to the whole program.

The first ~2 MW cost about **$37.5m per MW**. Extrapolating that initial cost mechanically to 30 MW would imply **~$1.125bn** in cumulative capex. **That is my sensitivity exercise, not management's 30-MW capex guidance**: future units may have a different CPU/GPU mix, generation and price.

At an illustrative 75% debt-funded proportion, $1.125bn of spending could imply ~$844m of additional gross borrowing; at 9% financing cost, that would add ~$76m of annual interest before principal repayments. These hypothetical assumptions show why **incremental adjusted EBITDA is not incremental FCF**. Hardware depreciation/renewal and asset-backed credit obligations also matter.

Management expects the new AI vector to contribute mainly from 2027 onward. I will not credit the full 30 MW economics until deployments are operating, contracted and producing acceptable cash returns.

## 5. Palantir, NVIDIA and the full-stack strategy

Rackspace's operating proposition is to integrate and run enterprise AI in production for clients that care about data residency, compliance, uptime, security and governance. A hyperscaler may sell compute; Palantir supplies governed data/AI software; AMD or NVIDIA supply accelerated hardware; **Rackspace aims to integrate, operate and remain accountable for the result**.

- **Palantir:** The July operating framework names Rackspace a preferred deployment and operations partner in selected regulated/sovereign contexts. The Q1 call described an early joint implementation completed in 41 days; July management disclosures referenced 400+ employee certifications. Q2 management described a small initial implementation followed by a potential expansion at the customer. Those are commercial signals, **not a disclosed EBITDA contribution**.
- **AMD:** A hardware/computing and joint commercial relationship underpinning the 30-MW planned capacity.
- **NVIDIA:** The September 2026 announcement of Rackspace's participation in the NVIDIA Cloud Partner Program and its Blackwell-based offering broadens hardware options. **It does not establish another AMD-sized contractual backlog**.
- **Rubrik / VMware / Uniphore / Dell:** Components of the broader resilient, virtualized and AI-enabled enterprise stack; partnership branding alone should not be valued as earnings.

**Competitive risk:** AWS, Azure, Google, specialist neoclouds, systems integrators and other data-center operators can compete aggressively. Rackspace's credible differentiation is existing regulated-client relationships plus managed, full-stack accountability; exclusivity or a sustainable moat remains unproven.

## 7. Debt, liquidity, FCF and the 2028 refinancing wall

At June 30, 2026:

| Measure | USD million |
|---|---:|
| Debt outstanding, contractual principal | 2,571.3 |
| Debt reported at carrying value | 2,786.7 |
| Cash and equivalents | 110.6 |
| **Net debt at carrying value (rounded)** | **~2,676 / ~2,700** |
| Revolver drawn | 260.0 |
| Total liquidity (cash + available credit) | 202.0 |

**Valuation convention:** I use **$2.7bn of net debt** for the scenario table below, as a rounded reference near the June 30 *carrying-value* debt less cash. This is not the same as subtracting contractual debt principal, fair market debt value or an all-in lease-adjusted enterprise claim. It is a simplifying, somewhat conservative equity-valuation convention. **Net debt can increase** substantially if equipment deployment uses incremental borrowing or operating cash flow disappoints. It could also decrease if ATM cash proceeds are retained or debt is repaid; the model does not assume that benefit automatically.

Large existing debt facilities and notes mature in **2028**. Rackspace repurchased some notes at a discount in H1, but increased its revolving borrowing; total contractual principal was actually higher in June than at December 2025. All relevant covenants were reported as met at Q2; compliance today does not ensure favorable refinancing tomorrow.

The $250m at-the-market (ATM) equity facility could protect liquidity or finance GPU-related spending but can materially dilute the stock. Hardware-backed funding may limit immediate cash use, but it still creates real financing obligations. A lender-financed asset is not the same as an asset paid for by internally generated FCF.

## 8. Dilution: share-count bridge underlying my valuation

I deliberately stress-test the equity valuation using **~356.5m shares (rounded to 357m)** rather than merely the weighted-average shares in the earnings guidance.

| Step | Share count / assumption |
|---|---:|
| Common shares outstanding at June 30, 2026 | 251.7m |
| Shares issued to Palantir on July 6 | +2.2m |
| Shares issued under the ATM through August 7 | +1.0m |
| **Documented shares after those two issuances** | **~254.9m** |
| Potential shares excluded from Q2 diluted EPS as anti-dilutive | +25.2m |
| Remaining assumed ATM authorization | ~$246m |
| **Hypothetical ATM sale price** (September 14, 2026 reference) | **$3.22** |
| Shares from fully placing assumed remaining ATM | **+~76.4m** |
| **Modeled fully diluted scenario shares** | **~356.5m (~357m)** |

Important qualifications:

- The **251.7m** base is reported in the Q2 filing; the **2.2m Palantir issuance** and **1.0m ATM issuance through August 7** are also documented. This does **not** certify that exactly 254.9m remained outstanding as of October 15: additional equity movements after August 7 must be checked in later SEC filings.
- The **25.2m** are potential common-share equivalents omitted from GAAP diluted EPS because they were anti-dilutive in the loss-making period. They include potentially issuable securities under compensation/other contingent arrangements, **not necessarily 25.2m vested shares that are certain to be issued**. Adding all of them is a stress-test assumption, not a prediction of the final GAAP diluted count.
- The **$246m** balance reflects the original ~$250m ATM less the roughly $4m raised through August 7. The actual remaining capacity or securities issued later may differ; the original filed proceeds were $4.1m **net** of fees while the ATM limit is **gross sale value**. The rounding does not change the illustrative conclusion.
- The **$3.22** price is a **September 14 reference assumed for all future issuance**, not an observed or promised ATM execution price. Issuing at a lower price increases dilution.
- I am not separately counting the reserve for possible future equity-plan grants on top of the 25.2m modeled potential shares, to avoid blindly double-counting overlapping securities.

## 10. Risks and quarterly monitoring

**Thesis-breaking or materially negative developments:**
- Delays in AMD deployment, inability to obtain equipment financing, or weak utilization.
- Commercial contracts signed at materially worse economics than the $15–20m/MW and 50%+ margin assumptions.
- New debt that grows faster than sustainable operating profits.
- Dilution materially beyond the 356.5m stress-test, especially at depressed prices.
- Private Cloud margin deterioration and Public Cloud exits not offset by new profits.
- Guidance FCF shortfall, sustained liquidity pressure, covenant stress or unfavorable 2028 refinancing.
- Hardware obsolescence, intense compute-price competition or regulatory/security failures.
- Legal exposure: the Q2 10-Q disclosed a shareholder class-action complaint concerning certain AI and business statements; allegations are contested and no adverse finding should be presumed.

**Quarterly monitoring checklist:** contracted vs deployed MW; annualized revenue per MW; gross and EBITDA margins on new deployments; AI share of RPO and RPO conversion; Private Cloud recurring organic growth; EBITDA-to-CFO bridge; cash-funded and lease-/debt-financed capex; debt principal, net debt and maturity extensions; current shares and cumulative ATM issuance.

## 11. Position sizing and provisional decision

**Classification: speculative special situation, not a core holding.** I would research a small initial exposure rather than underwrite the $800m EBITDA outcome as inevitable. As a purely indicative framework at unchanged fundamentals, **$2.50–3.25** would warrant closer review for an initial position; a price below that is not necessarily cheap if liquidity/refinancing risk has deteriorated. An illustrative initial sizing of **~0.5–1.0% of a diversified portfolio**, with an upper research limit of **~2% only after execution improves**, reflects the real chance of permanent equity impairment. These are *conditional research parameters*, not evidence of an actual position.

**Decision rule:** I would increase conviction only when contracted capacity, cash conversion and capital structure improve together. A stock-price collapse by itself is not a buy signal; another impressive partnership announcement by itself is not proof of value creation.
