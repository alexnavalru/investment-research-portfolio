# Amentum (AMTM) - Deleveraging & Capital Allocation

**Case type:** Watchlist / Deleveraging / Capital Allocation Catalyst  
**Status:** Watchlist; prospective entry around **$18/share**  
**Target price:** **above $25/share**  
**Primary skills:** leverage analysis, FCF valuation, backlog analysis, capital allocation, special-situation/tax-structure analysis

## Business overview

Amentum (NYSE: AMTM) is a global engineering and technology services contractor focused on **defense, intelligence, aerospace, nuclear energy and critical infrastructure**. It primarily serves U.S. government agencies (including the Department of Defense and NASA), allied governments and other mission-critical customers through long-term contracts. Today's listed company was formed on **September 27, 2024**, when Legacy Amentum combined with Jacobs' Critical Mission Solutions (CMS) business in a Reverse Morris Trust transaction.

## Investment thesis and potential catalysts

1. **Low valuation and re-rating potential:** Near **$18.50/share**, my FY2026 model implies **~7.9x adjusted earnings, ~8.0x FCF and ~7.1x EV/EBITDA**. The valuation work points toward **more than $25/share** if execution and capital allocation improve; a higher multiple is possible, not assured.
2. **Substantial backlog:** **$48.2bn of backlog (+8% YoY)** and **1.3x LTM book-to-bill** underpin multi-year demand visibility, despite current revenue pressure from contract transitions and divestitures. Backlog includes unfunded work and options, not guaranteed sales.
3. **Deleveraging:** Net leverage has fallen to **3.0x**, ahead of management's prior timetable, with **~$3.42bn net debt** at Q3 FY2026. Further deleveraging could reduce interest costs, strengthen EPS and improve balance-sheet flexibility.
4. **Buyback and capital-allocation catalyst:** The **two-year Reverse Morris Trust tax-related restriction period ended September 27, 2026**, lifting a significant obstacle to repurchases and certain transactions. Management has discussed **buybacks, selective M&A and further debt reduction** with the Board. At today's low multiples, repurchases could be highly accretive, but **none have been assumed or promised**.
5. **Nuclear growth optionality:** Existing expertise in nuclear engineering, decommissioning and advanced reactors positions Amentum for a possible global investment cycle. Evidence includes a **$207m Dutch nuclear-program contract**, a **$406m UK SMR engineering contract**, and **over $400m of Q3 advanced-nuclear bookings**. This is upside **beyond my base valuation**, not a guaranteed growth rate.
6. **Significant sponsor ownership:** Private-equity sponsors **Lindsay Goldberg and American Securities own ~36% combined**, with Board representation. Their substantial economic exposure supports alignment, although future sponsor share sales remain a risk.
7. **FCF per-share upside:** My model projects **~$571m FCF for FY2026** and **~$595m for FY2027**. Stabilizing operations, continued debt reduction and potentially well-priced buybacks could lift **FCF/share and EPS**, even without strong near-term revenue growth.

## Why the Reverse Morris Trust Matters

Amentum's merger with Jacobs' CMS closed on **September 27, 2024**. To preserve its intended tax-free treatment, the related tax agreement restricted **share repurchases, certain major mergers/ownership changes and asset disposals** during a **two-year period ending September 27, 2026**, subject to exceptions.

**That specific restriction period has now ended**, improving the Board's flexibility to consider buybacks, M&A or debt repayment. **It does not guarantee repurchases**, nor does it eliminate all continuing tax-related obligations or transaction risks. FY2025 Form 10-K.

## Deleveraging Is Reaching an Inflection Point

Amentum spent the first phase after the transaction prioritizing debt reduction.

At Q3 FY2026:

- gross debt was approximately **$3.875bn**;
- cash was approximately **$459m**;
- net debt was therefore about **$3.42bn**;
- net leverage had fallen to **3.0x**, one quarter ahead of management's prior target;
- management expected leverage to fall **below 3.0x in Q4**.

This matters because the capital-allocation discussion has already changed. On the Q3 FY2026 call, management said it was working with the Board on a broader set of options including **M&A, share repurchases and continued debt reduction**.

That makes late 2026 / FY2027 a potentially important transition from:

**deleveraging first**

to:

**deleveraging + opportunistic capital returns / investment**

## Backlog Supports the Fundamental Case

Amentum's backlog remains one of the strongest parts of the thesis.

At Q3 FY2026:

- total backlog was **$48.2bn**, up roughly **8% year over year**;
- funded backlog was approximately **$6.2bn**, up about **11% year over year**;
- quarterly book-to-bill was **1.1x**;
- trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill was **1.3x**.

The backlog is not equivalent to guaranteed revenue - it contains unfunded work and contract options - but it provides evidence that the business continues to win work even while reported revenue is affected by contract transitions, divestitures and selected exits from low-return programs.

## Nuclear Energy Exposure: A Long-Term Growth Option

One part of Amentum that I find particularly attractive is its exposure to the **nuclear-energy value chain**.

This is not a thesis that Amentum itself is a nuclear utility or that it directly benefits from electricity prices. The opportunity is that Amentum already has engineering, program-management, decommissioning and nuclear-services capabilities that position it to participate in a larger global nuclear build-out.

Recent contract activity supports that view:

- in **January 2026**, an Amentum-led consortium won a contract worth up to **$207m** to support the Netherlands' new nuclear-build program;
- in **April 2026**, an Amentum-led joint venture won a **$406m, up-to-14-year** contract as owner's engineer for the UK's first small modular reactor program;
- in Q3 FY2026, Amentum said it had booked **more than $400m** for initial engineering, development and design of advanced nuclear technologies;
- in **September 2026**, an Amentum joint venture was selected as preferred supplier for a Sellafield framework worth up to **$2.78bn**;
- Amentum is also working with Rolls-Royce SMR on deployment of its first SMRs in the UK and Czech Republic.

I view this as a **long-duration growth option** rather than something required to justify the current valuation. My broader industry view is that demand for reliable low-carbon baseload power, grid resilience and energy-intensive infrastructure such as data centers can support greater nuclear investment over time. Amentum's existing capabilities and recent contract wins give it a credible position if that investment cycle accelerates.

The key investment point is therefore:

**low current valuation + existing backlog + deleveraging + potential buybacks + underappreciated nuclear growth optionality**

## Private-Equity Ownership

Amentum also has an unusual shareholder structure.

**Lindsay Goldberg** and **American Securities**, the private-equity sponsors behind Legacy Amentum, remain very large shareholders. The company's 2026 proxy showed each group beneficially owning roughly **18%** of the company, or approximately **36% combined**.

I view that concentration as relevant because these owners have significant economic exposure to the equity value of Amentum and Board representation.

That alignment does not guarantee shareholder-friendly decisions, and the sponsors have registration rights that can facilitate future sales. The relevant point for the thesis is simply that two sophisticated financial sponsors still have substantial capital tied to the equity outcome.

## Potential Buyback Catalyst

This is the key optionality.

Amentum has not announced a large repurchase program in the thesis as of this update. However:

- leverage is now around the company's target threshold;
- management has explicitly discussed share repurchases as one of the options under Board review;
- the specific two-year RMT tax restrictions have expired; and
- the stock is trading at a low multiple of modeled FCF and earnings.

At a low valuation, buybacks could be particularly accretive to FCF and EPS per share.

My model includes an illustrative scenario in which approximately **$200m** is deployed into repurchases around **$22/share**, partially offset by stock-option dilution. This is a scenario, **not management guidance**.

## Position Construction

**Current position:** none.

**Prospective entry:** approximately **$18/share**.

I would treat this as a staged entry rather than a binary catalyst trade because the timing and size of any repurchase program are uncertain.

## Key Risks

- Revenue pressure from NASA or other major customer programs.
- Backlog converting more slowly than expected into revenue.
- Government-budget and procurement delays.
- Margin expansion failing to offset slower top-line growth.
- M&A being prioritized over buybacks at unattractive valuations.
- Private-equity sponsors selling down their stakes and creating an overhang.
- Buybacks not being authorized despite the expiry of the RMT restrictions.
- Higher-for-longer leverage or weaker FCF conversion.
- Nuclear projects being delayed, cancelled or subject to budget, regulatory or execution risk.
- Nuclear contract wins not translating into attractive incremental margins or cash flow.

## What This Case Demonstrates

This case combines traditional fundamental analysis with a corporate-structure catalyst:

**low valuation → deleveraging → growing backlog → removal of RMT constraints → broader capital-allocation optionality**

The investment case does not require a buyback to work, but a well-priced repurchase program could materially improve the per-share return profile.
