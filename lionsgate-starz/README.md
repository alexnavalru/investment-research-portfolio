# Lionsgate / Starz — Spin-Off & Special Situation

**Case type:** Special Situation / Spin-Off  
**Primary skills:** SOTP valuation, precedent transactions, catalyst analysis, trade construction, downside and timing risk

## Executive Summary

The core opportunity came from the separation of Lionsgate Studios from Starz. Before the spin-off, both the standalone studio and the holding company traded publicly. At times, the standalone studio traded at a higher equity value than the holding company that also owned Starz.

That pricing relationship implied that the market was assigning a **negative value to Starz**.

The situation created a potential relative-value structure: long the holding company and short the standalone studio in order to isolate Starz exposure at an implied negative price. The spin-off acted as the corporate catalyst that could force the market to price the two assets independently.

## Variant Perception

The thesis was not that Starz was a high-quality asset. The thesis was that the market price appeared inconsistent with the cash generation and strategic value of the assets being separated.

At the time of the analysis:

- Lionsgate Studios generated approximately **$450m of EBITDA** and traded around **10x–11x EBITDA**.
- Relevant studio precedent transactions had occurred at materially higher multiples.
- Starz generated approximately **$1.4bn of revenue**, **$200m of EBITDA** and about **$100m of levered FCF**, while its implied market value was extremely low.
- The separation increased strategic optionality because the studio could be evaluated independently in a future M&A scenario.

## Valuation Framework

### Lionsgate Studios

I used precedent media transactions as a valuation reference rather than assuming the highest observed multiple was applicable.

Using approximately **16x EBITDA** on roughly **$450m of EBITDA** implied an enterprise value around **$7.2bn**. After adjusting for debt, the resulting equity value was materially above the market value observed at the time.

### Starz

Starz was a structurally weaker business operating in a challenged television environment, so I did not assign it a premium multiple. The key point was that the market appeared to be pricing it near zero — and at times effectively below zero — despite positive EBITDA and cash flow.

## Catalyst

The principal catalyst was the completion of the spin-off.

The separation mattered because it:

- removed the conglomerate structure;
- forced separate market prices for the studio and Starz;
- increased strategic optionality for the studio;
- made the valuation discrepancy easier for the market to observe.

## Position Construction

The cleanest theoretical structure was long the holding company and short the standalone studio.

Because the opportunity became clear relatively late and available capital was limited, the actual implementation used options. Initially, only short-dated options were available. After the separation, longer-dated Lionsgate Studios calls became available and were used to obtain exposure with a longer thesis horizon.

A later long-dated call position was initiated when Lionsgate Studios traded around **$6** and exited around **$11.50**. The approximately **5x** return on that specific option position is less important than the process: valuation, catalyst, instrument selection and timing all had to align.

## Key Risks

- Delay or cancellation of the spin-off.
- Further deterioration in Starz.
- Volatility in studio EBITDA and film releases.
- Precedent M&A multiples proving inapplicable.
- Short borrow and spread risk in the relative-value structure.
- Option expiration occurring before the thesis materialized.
- The market continuing to assign a conglomerate discount after separation.

**market pricing → segment economics → valuation → corporate action → trade construction → risk management**
