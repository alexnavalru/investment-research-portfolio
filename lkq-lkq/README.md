# LKQ Corporation (LKQ) — Restructuring, Deleveraging & Strategic Review

**Research date:** 13 October 2026  
**Status:** Watchlist / active research  
**Preferred entry:** around **$20/share**  
**Price target:** **none established**  
**Case:** Special situation / operational turnaround  
**Reference share price:** ~$22.10

## 1. Business overview
LKQ is a global distributor of replacement vehicle parts, primarily serving professional repair shops.

- **North America:** distributes aftermarket, recycled and remanufactured mechanical and collision-repair parts to repair shops and collision networks.
- **Europe:** distributes mechanical replacement parts and private-label products to garages through its wholesale and logistics network.
- **Specialty:** distributes accessories and specialized products for RVs, towing, marine, vehicle performance and related markets.

LKQ sold its **Self-Service** salvage business in 2025.

## 2. Investment thesis and potential catalysts

The thesis combines **portfolio simplification, operational restructuring and FCF-funded deleveraging**. A sale of the entire company offers additional upside, but **a takeover is not necessary** for the investment to work.

1. **Activist involvement and governance (February–October 2025):** Ancora and Engine Capital secured board representation in February; Ananym subsequently pushed LKQ to unlock value through the sale or separation of Europe.
2. **Portfolio simplification and strategic review (2025–2026):** after divesting **Self-Service** in 2025, LKQ launched a **Specialty sale process** in December and a broader strategic review in January 2026, including possible divisional disposals or a **sale of the whole company**. Proceeds could help reduce debt, but no deal or sale price is assumed.
3. **Restructuring and cost savings (2026–2027):** procurement, efficiencies and a simpler operating structure could gradually improve profitability without requiring stronger end-market demand.
4. **European recovery:** Germany's ERP stabilization, better service levels and higher private-label penetration could restore lost revenue and improve European margins.
5. **Deleveraging through FCF:** directing cash generation to debt rather than expensive buybacks could improve the balance sheet and reduce equity risk. My SOTP assumes **only $350m net debt reduction from Q2 2026 to year-end**.
6. **Optional dividend reallocation:** cutting the dividend could initially hurt the stock but free cash for faster debt repayment; this is **not my base case**.
7. **Potential SOTP re-rating:** a clearer portfolio and improved segment performance could narrow the valuation discount. This is a **valuation scenario, not my price target**.

**Who are the activists?** **Ancora** and **Engine Capital** are activist investors that negotiated LKQ board representation in February 2025. **Ananym Capital** is a New York activist fund founded by **Charlie Penner and Alex Silver**; Penner previously worked at JANA Partners. Ananym has argued that separating or selling European assets could unlock value. Activist pressure increases the focus on capital allocation, but does **not** guarantee a transaction.

**Earnings-call evidence:** Q1 2026 management targeted **>$50m annual cost savings** and expected improved European private-label pricing into 2027. Q2 stressed cost/productivity gains hidden by the German ERP failure: management estimated **~$140m of lost Q2 German revenue and ~$50m EBITDA**, while the UK/Benelux remained weak. German sales run-rate was reported **>85% of normal by late July**, with a stated ambition to approach **100% near year-end**; this is a management *goal*, not a proven outcome.

## 3. Operating problems and capital allocation challenges
1. **Post-Covid normalization:** unusually strong 2021–22 parts demand/margins unwound; claims, repair volumes and consumers remain pressured. North American Q2 +0.5% organic Parts & Services sales was **mainly price**, not a true volume recovery.
2. **Debt:** roughly **$3.961bn gross / $3.660bn net** at Q2 2026; management's reported leverage was about **2.8x**. Replacing a $500m term loan with revolver borrowing in July is **refinancing, not debt elimination**. Keep investment-grade headroom.
3. **Poor prior capital allocation:** 2021–22 saw **~$1.917bn buybacks** near much higher market valuations. Shares fell, but today the lost flexibility is more obvious. Dividends started in 2021 and absorbed ~$310m in 2025. At ~$660m scenario FCF, payout is manageable (~47%) and I do **not** expect a cut in my central case; a serious FCF deterioration and **dividend cut could still cause a sharp share-price fall**.
4. **Europe / ERP execution:** 30+ legacy systems after many acquisitions; Germany migration in April 2026 hurt availability, fill rates and customer share of wallet. Q2 Europe organic Parts & Services revenue was **-12.6%**. Delayed recovery risks permanent leakage to competitors and another guidance cut.
5. **Execution elsewhere:** UK pricing competition, deliberate exit from low-margin Benelux accounts, U.S. paint weakness, tariff/freight costs, and Specialty margins. Future ERP rollouts in 2027 carry repeat-risk.
6. **Governance:** current CEO is an LKQ veteran, not a complete outsider; 2025 total CEO grant-date pay is high relative to direct stock ownership. Most institutional owners are passive/large asset managers. Insider transactions show **some small buys, not a major insider accumulation programme**.

**DSO** measures customer collection days.  
**DIO** measures inventory holding days.  
**DPO** measures supplier payment days.  
**CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO**.  
Working capital is trade receivables **+** inventory **−** trade payables. All days are based on my model conventions.

**Why it matters:** LKQ reduced **WC/revenue from 26.9% to 18.5%** and **CCC from 124 to 90 days** (2017–2025). But the improvement came mainly from **paying suppliers later (DPO: 44 → 85 days)**, not from selling inventory faster (**DIO: 133 → 144 days**). If supplier payment terms tighten, LKQ may need more cash for working capital, reducing FCF.

## 6. Valuation — own model, competitors and SOTP

With FY2026 $1.410bn adjusted EBITDA: **~6.6x EV/EBITDA**. At FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint $2.75, **~8.0x P/E**. At $660m adjusted model FCF, **~8.5x P/FCF**, or **~11.8% FCF yield**. 

**Historic drawdown and entry discipline:** LKQ has declined **~58.8% from its peak**, among its steepest historical drawdowns, surpassed by **2008 (~62.8%)** and **2020 (~68.0%)** in the referenced series. Yet a historic fall does **not** make the shares automatically cheap. At the **$22.10 reference price** and **$660m FY2026E FCF (CFO − CAPEX)**, LKQ trades at **~8.5x P/FCF** or **~14.0x EV/FCF including net debt**; EV/FCF is only a leverage-aware cross-check because CFO already includes interest. **I do not see an obvious bargain: my preferred entry is around $20, with no price target.** A whole-company sale is speculative; restructuring and deleveraging could unlock value without it. The **$22.6 conservative SOTP after assumed H2 debt reduction** is a conditional valuation scenario, **not a target price**.

## 7. Key risks and what to monitor

1. **Q3 results and entry timing (29 October 2026):** another guidance cut or weak cash conversion could push shares lower, potentially offering an entry near **$20**. I may wait for these results.
2. **German ERP recovery:** monitor service levels, recovered customers and European margins. Continued disruption could cause lasting market-share losses.
3. **U.S. demand and macro:** weak collision-repair volumes, paint sales and higher interest rates could pressure growth, profits and financing costs.
4. **FCF and debt reduction:** monitor delivery of my **~$660m FY2026 FCF** assumption and whether net debt actually falls, rather than merely being refinanced.
5. **Strategic review:** track the potential Specialty or whole-company sale, net proceeds, separation costs and taxes. **No transaction is guaranteed**.
6. **Working capital:** inventory is rising and cash conversion depends heavily on longer supplier payment terms (**DPO**). A reversal could reduce FCF.
7. **Dividend risk:** a cut could initially hurt the share price, but redirecting cash to debt repayment might strengthen LKQ long term. **Not my base case**.
